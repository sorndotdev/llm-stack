## Landing Page & Visual Patterns

### Website-as-Salesperson Model (The Call Order)
- The page, not a person, must run the full sales conversation — "the best case scenario is that your website completely replaces you as a salesperson" — because it gets no feedback and cannot tailor live: "you can hear the feedback. You can tailor. But your website cannot tailor... So, how do we accommodate for that?" The maturity model: live seller → flattened page → self-segmenting quiz. Build flat first: "level one... no fancy stuff... a flat landing page."
- Each block answers one silent question in call order: hook ("what's in it for me?") → benefit stack ("what do I get?") → objection removal ("why won't this fail for me?") → authority/social proof ("why believe you?") → feature stack → CTA ("what do I do now?"). Price and application come after benefit proof, never before; the failure signature is "I'm great. Now pay me."
- Features sit near the bottom as completeness, not persuasion (see Feature De-Bloat — the "feature stack" quote and the rotate-toward-benefit rule live there). The VSL carries the same sequence — "there's nothing special about a video other than... video does a better job of conveying this information." Logic before design: "I think about the logic first... then you can make it pretty at the end without losing that logic."
- Infer, don't ask: never ask a question you can answer for the user — default the common case (language, location, currency) and put the exception in a small option ("why don't you just assume it's in English and then have a little option at the bottom"). Every avoidable question is friction before the ask.
- Four-register page audit: a page cannot read the visitor, so it must carry all four registers — social (pictures of people), practical (logical explanations), emotional (colors and a feel that evokes safe/comfortable), action ("here, buy now"). Apple is the canonical page: "It has social, they have pictures of people. It has, for the practical people, very logical explanations. It has, for those emotional people, colors and a feel that evokes safe... And it has that kind of action for those people who are more aggressive. It's like, here, buy now." A page missing a register speaks only to that type's share; add the missing entry point.

### Long-Form Asset Sequence (The 18-Step Spine)
- The full order for any long-form asset: hook → quick bribe → authority → central thesis → dimensionalized rewards → early proof → urgency → return to thesis and reward → pacing the pain → product → value build (testimonials + benefit stack) → trivialize price → bonuses → trivialize price again → scarcity → CTA → guarantee → FAQ. Each block answers the question the previous one raises; the product arrives only after desire and belief are built.
- The compressed five-step commercial form: hook → problem → connection/authority → solution → product + CTA.
- Front-end slots: hand over a small tool right after the hook ("all of these have tools"), and show a named insider group already living the promise before the first hard claim — reciprocity and proof before authority and thesis, both kept short.
- Modulate between reward imagery and knowledge beats to reset attention (the garage ad: Lamborghini → bookshelves → "this is a reminder" → $47 backstory → mentors → CTA); end on guarantee, then FAQ.

### Funnel Page Anatomy
- Measured across 13 funnel pages at 4 viewports (2560×1440, 1440×900, 768×1024, 390×844): 12 flow pages (4 of them program pages) plus an app landing page. Hero-only two-column pairs appear on 5 of the 13 pages.
- Canonical skeleton: short headline → medium headline → shorter subheadline → medium-to-long video → long copy (what's included, case studies, testimonials). The measured pages approximate it: hero (H1 → subhead → media → CTA) → proof near the top → 5–15 argument sections → offer/value stack → application or next-step CTA → FAQ/testimonial block → legal footer. Hero and CTA inside the first viewport: all 13 at 2560×1440, falling to 7 at 1440×900.
- Frequencies: hero or near-top video on 10 of the 13 (3 embeds currently dead); proof block within the first two screens on 4; long letter sections on 8; FAQ accordion visible on 4; testimonial wall visible on 5; multi-step application form on 4; no page shows a checkout with payment fields; legal footer on all 12 flow pages, with the identical 323-word legal block on the 4 program pages.
- Conversion is never a checkout: a multi-step modal (7, 7, 9 and 8 steps across the four), a third-party registration modal, a 2-field waitlist, or a link to the next funnel step. "Step 1 of N" plus a thin progress bar appears on the four multi-step forms as a qualification pattern; the no-checkout handoff suits application-gated, high-ticket offers, not self-serve purchase flows. On-page order bumps/upsells: none of the 13; one copy-only upsell mention.
- Template 1 — modern white letter (2 of the 13): Georgia H1/H2, Montserrat or Arial body, green #25652F CTA, 800–820px column, 17,654–22,333px tall at desktop.
- Template 2 — legacy black hero-card (3 of the 13): full-bleed photo hero card inside a 940–970px column, Gotham headings, orange #C97600 two-line CTA, white or dark panels. One program-page variant runs the same system full-bleed.
- Template 3 — per-offer engine pages (8 of the 13, including the app landing page): one shared funnel backend, but typeface, accent colour and section rhythm change page to page. The program subset shares chrome — logo header, click-to-call, 15-link footer, identical legal block — and still disagrees on typography.
- The authority system repeats more reliably than the layout: featured-at logo walls 3 of the 13, ChatGPT-ranking screenshots 3, named-client photos or case lines 2, raw message-screenshot testimonial walls 2, Wistia video testimonials 1. Proof is assembled from whatever is cheap and current, not from one template.
- Current-state caveat: dead YouTube embeds ("account terminated") and legacy sections hidden with `display:none` appear at every viewport; visible-element counts stay flat across widths on 11 of the 13, so the hidden content is decay, not responsive hiding. The pages are captured as they sit today, not at their peak. Where the spoken rules conflict with these measured pages, the pages win.

### Layout Geometry
- Text measure 518–772px on the modern and legacy letter templates; 518px is the most repeated body measure across the legacy and program pages. Program/white-label letters widen to 750–1,000px. The column holds across widths: 748→716→350px and 772→720→342px on the two modern letters, 810→728→350px on the longest letter.
- Outer container max-width 800–1,350px at desktop: 800–820px on the modern letter, 850–1,200px on the engine pages, 1,260–1,350px on the program pages, 1,170px on the app page. Content centers with 15–26px side padding at 1,164px; the modern letter leaves ~182px of margin on each side of its 748px column.
- Section padding 42–46px top/bottom on the modern letter sections (42 on one, 46 on the other); 15–50px on engine/program sections, with some program sections at 0; hero padding 6–100px. Padding is the least responsive token: unchanged across widths on 11 of the 13, with an engine form card's 20/45→10px at 390 the main exception.
- Page heights are bimodal at desktop: 2,411–8,872px for squeeze, registration and short program pages (8 of the 13), and 17,654–25,205px for long letters (5 of the 13). At 390px the same pages reach 3,677–40,662px, 48–67% taller than at 1440×900; growth is stacking and footer expansion, not new content.
- Hero media geometry: VSL 694–772px wide on the funnel letters (16:9, 348–434px tall), 474–750px on the engine pages; legacy hero card 940px wide with 20/100px padding and 15px radius; squeeze form card 460×398 white on light gray with a soft shadow. Hero video is fluid on 9 of the 13; two engine pages hold a fixed 560×315 iframe until 390.
- Long letters alternate white and warm-gray bands (rgb 247,247,241 on the modern letter); the legacy template stacks white boxes; the longest letter uses rules and no cards at all; the full-bleed program variant uses one 1,140px dark panel over a full-bleed CSS photo. Backgrounds separate sections; borders rarely do.
- The pattern: a narrow measure plus generous vertical padding turns a 2,600-word letter into 18,000–25,000px of scroll. Height is bought with whitespace and proof images, not copy density. Keep the geometry, not the page length.
- Cut duplicate chrome above the fold: "what is the point of having two logos there?" Every above-fold pixel should carry the promise, the media, or the ask.
- Spacing is gap-free: sections stack at 0px margins — the visible rhythm is the sum of adjacent paddings. 42px top/bottom on the modern letter (84px content rhythm), 50px on the legacy bands (100px content gap; 40px at phone), 10–40px margins-only on the squeeze. Symmetric padding, never asymmetric.
- No strict grid: padding values are off-grid and em-derived (42, 27, 18, 22, 31, 38 on the modern letter; near-5px steps on the legacy). Phone rhythm compresses to 20–34px.
- No vh-sized sections: heroes run 68.7–98.8% of a 900px viewport as fixed px (618px squeeze, 768px modern letter, 889px legacy card); everything below is content-sized.

### Typography
- H1 33–51px at desktop (51.2px on the largest engine-page H1; the app page's 106.6px is a brand-splash outlier), weight 300–900 (600–900 on the sales letters), centered, line-height 1.10–1.47; mobile H1 24–35px. H2 20–38px desktop, 16–30px mobile. H3/H4 17–28px, sometimes used as body copy (the 28px benefit statements on the legacy hero-card pages). Body 14–25px depending on template, 16–22px on most sales pages; body line-height 1.38–1.88, dominant 1.60–1.70.
- Body size rises with the offer: 16px on the $1 page, 18px on the $47 and $9/mo pages, 19px on the $497 page, 22px on the event and workshop pages. Larger type accompanies higher consideration, not lower.
- Pairings cluster: Georgia headings + Montserrat or Arial body (modern letter); Gotham throughout (legacy and webinar pages); Lato 900 headings + Georgia body (the longest letter); Oswald + Helvetica (one engine page); Archivo Black + Lato + Noto Sans (the app page). The typefaces change; the scale does not.
- The app page's Archivo Black "COMING SOON" H1 is a fluid brand-splash headline, not the house scale: 106.6px at 1440/2560, 86px at 1,164, 56.8px at 768, 35.1px at 390. Treat 33–51px as the working sales-page H1 range.
- Long letters carry 17–19 H2s; headings are section labels, not a hierarchy. A page can run 5–15 argument sections with no H3 between them.
- CTA typography is uppercase and tracked on the engine pages (20px/900 with 2px letter-spacing on one; 20px/900 with 1.2px on another), uppercase 18px/700 on the modern green buttons, and two-line on the legacy orange buttons.
- Colour does hierarchy work the headings do not: red H2s on one modern letter and black H2s on the other, yellow H2s inside the dark legacy hero card, red countdown numerals on an otherwise black-and-white engine page. One accent per page, applied to the CTA and the second-most-important element.
- Small print, eyebrows and legal run 14–18px; the 323-word legal block is the only sustained text below body size.
- Deliberate scale breaks carry proof: red 17px underlined captions under ranking screenshots (one modern letter), 28px H3-as-body benefit lines, a 930×50 pull-quote card rendered as a background image (one engine page).
- Semantic inversion to avoid: on one engine page the visually dominant headline is a `<p>` at 36px and the only `<h1>` is the 22px testimonial-section label; another engine page has no H1 at all, only a quoted H2.

### Text Chunking
- Headlines run 6–37 words. 12–37 is the direct-response letter/squeeze range (37 on the longest letter); 9–11 the modern event and coaching heroes; 6–16 the program pages. Subheads 3–34 words, usually mechanism + effort removal ("where you learn by watching him work").
- Paragraph counts swing with page purpose: 9 on the $47 page, 36–75 on the letter pages, 129 on the most paragraph-heavy engine page. Average paragraph 21–34 words on the letter pages, median 21–33; one-sentence letters average 15 (one engine letter: 61 paragraphs, mostly one sentence); the sparse app landing page averages 66 because it has only nine paragraphs.
- Long or none under the video: short copy beneath a VSL competes with it and loses both ways. Write thousands of words or nothing — a 27-page scrolling page produced six figures — and the measured letter pages follow it (36–129 paragraphs); the sparse pages that break it (the app page 9 paragraphs, one engine feature page 21) are feature pages, not VSL letters.
- Length calibrates to tangibility: pre-understood products run short, abstract or novel promises must be taught and run long — "it needs to be as long as it needs to be" (observed one to 120 minutes). The grandma test is the length signal: "if you try to tell your grandma that, she's not going to understand, so you're going to need a longer video sales letter."
- Serve readers and skimmers on the same page: a contrasting-colour highlight layer over the full text, with the highlighted words alone fitting the above-fold budget. One case is a 3–4k-word student page that converted at 8.5% on cold Facebook traffic on $1k spend for a $37 product; the measured pages approximate it with red H2s and captions on the modern letters rather than inline highlights.
- Facts exist to give emotion permission: the 20/80 composition rule is "almost perfectly... 20 of the page is facts, 80 is just emotion." The long letters run closer to 30/70 (proof screenshots, curriculum, price math) but sequence desire first and evidence second.
- Most paragraphs are 1–3 sentences. The longest non-legal paragraph is 60–81 words; the only 300+ word blocks are legal boilerplate. The most paragraph-heavy engine page has 26 of 129 paragraphs over three sentences; one modern letter 7 of 56.
- Bullet lists run 1–10 items at 5–44 words each; checkmark tables 8–9 rows; numbered value stacks 10 items. Lists absorb detail that would otherwise become paragraphs — deliverables, curriculum, "10 commandments", bonus titles.
- The fold is an attention budget, not a design boundary — and the budget is bigger than the ~15-word folklore: measured pages run 15–299 above-fold words across the four viewports (28–287 at 1440×900; 15–106 at 390 on 12 of the 13 pages, the one short page at 287 because it fits whole). Video-led folds show 28–106 words; text/form-led folds 100–299. The rule that survives: strongest claim or proof first, never a neutral greeting; below the fold can run as long as the copy earns.
- The driver is hero media, not price: video-led folds hold few words, text and form folds hold many. The modern pages deliberately show almost no copy above the fold and let the video carry the argument.
- Two letter pages open with "Dear Friend," a ribbon or salutation band, and 6–8 one-line paragraphs before the first list; the one-sentence paragraph is the default rhythm on two engine pages, not an occasional device.
- Every page places its conversion action inside the first ~1,285px of desktop scroll — a CTA at y 290–996 or a form card at y 509–1,086 — but only 42 of the 52 page-viewport combinations keep it fully visible (see Responsive).

### Copy Taxonomy (Four Buckets)
- Every bullet is one of four things — feature, benefit, mechanism, or objection-removal — and each belongs in its own section: "ask yourself every single bullet point: is it a feature or a benefit? If it's a feature, low priority, stack the benefits, then make your benefits congruent with a specific hook."
- Objection-removal ("money-back guarantee") is needed "but it's not in this section"; "who this is for or isn't" is not a benefit either. Impure bullets exist — "become super affiliate partner with Tai Lopez himself... this is the mechanism. You see, it's not pure" — name the impurity rather than pretending.
- Name each benefit as a proprietary-sounding "factor" so it reads as a discovered lever rather than a promise: "it's the deserve-it factor. Discover the front-loading factor." Keep the factor tied to a real behavior; the name is a carrier, not a substitute for the mechanism.

### Text-to-Image Ratio
- First-viewport media share 3.5–55.7% across the 52 page-viewport combinations: 10–31% at 1440×900, 3.5–19.7% at 2560×1440, 6.5–55.7% at 768×1024, 4.8–53.1% at 390×844. It peaks at the 768 iPad width on 7 of the 13 pages, at 390 on three engine pages (53.1%, 19.6%, 28.4%) and at 1440 on three legacy hero-card pages (23.2%, 21.9%, 30.8%).
- Whole-page media share 2.6–78.2% across viewports: 4–57% at 1440×900, with most pages at 2.6–30% and one engine page the outlier at 57–78% because its second half is a 15-screenshot testimonial wall. Several pages use full-bleed CSS background photos not counted, so true media share is higher.
- 116–190 words per media asset on the letter pages and 181–254 on the longest pages; 29–347 across the full set once logos and icons are counted (median roughly 70–180). Media is proof or product, never decoration: logo walls, ChatGPT ranking screenshots, raw testimonial screenshots, product mockups, one author cutout. Proof over decoration.
- Reading is interrupted on a cadence: an image roughly every 300 words on the densest engine letter, every 1,500–2,500px on the other long letters. The testimonial wall is the one place where images outnumber text.
- Testimonial screenshots render 333–361px wide and 83–782px tall in a 3-column masonry; the varying aspect ratios are kept uncropped, which is what makes them read as raw captures rather than designed cards.
- Image quality is not the point: the app page upscales a 300×61 logo to 470×95, and one engine page upscales 141×93 arrows to 190×125. The proof value is in what the image shows.
- The poster is the first impression: a composed face or expression image, never a mid-speech frame ("it looks like you're caught in mid-"). The measured posters are mostly composed speaker shots or designed title cards; the longest letter's three-part thumbnail is the most designed.
- Video durations 0:51–43:29 (0:51 squeeze, 2:11 event, 7:02 ads letter, 17:53 private mentoring, 24:47 test group, 43:29 coaching); all are 16:9 except one legacy page's 2.09:1 custom player, and all are paused with a poster. None autoplays on a live page.
- First-screen composition splits by template: the modern letters center a 748–772px video under the headline; the legacy hero card holds the headline, video, offer bar and CTA inside one 940px photo card; the engine pages put video left and copy right, or video centered with the form below. The CTA always shares the first screen with the video or form at 2560×1440.
- Caveat: fold shares now exist at all four viewports, and they are non-monotonic. Treat each as a page-geometry fact for that viewport, not as universal fold behavior; the mobile measurements used CDP device metrics at DPR 1, so real-device chrome can shift the fold.

### CTAs
- 2–5 repeated CTAs on most pages; the longest direct-response letter repeats 10. One action and one label per page, never varied within the page ("Join The Test Group", "GRAB YOUR FREE SPOT", "YES, BUILD MY 3 ADS NOW").
- Button geometry 44–86px tall (54–86 on the funnel templates), 199–534px wide, radius 0–50px, label 14–28px. Modern template: 54–64px tall, 243–512px wide, radius 8px. Legacy: 76–82px tall, two-line label (22–28px main + 14px sub), radius 3px. Program pages: 44–65px flat bars.
- At 390px the primary button spans the content column (284–360px) and can wrap to two lines (338×98 on one engine page); the longest letter's green bar leaves only a sliver above the fold at 390.
- One accent colour carries every CTA on a page: green (modern), orange (legacy), red (one engine page), blue (four engine pages), and no secondary button competing for the click except one engine page's two outline tier buttons.
- Placement tracks page length: repeat roughly every 1,000–2,500px of scroll on 18k–25k letters; 2–4 evenly spaced on 4k–9k pages; a single hero form on the 2.6k squeeze. The longest letter repeats its CTA 10 times from y 970 to 19,765 — the clearest cadence rule in the set.
- Placement also tracks price strategy: pages that show a price in the hero put the CTA directly under the offer strip; pages that withhold price put the first CTA under the video and the price CTA 10k–23k px later.
- First CTA position is y 290–996 across the desktop set; two engine pages scroll to an embedded form card, the two modern letters open a 7-step modal, one engine page opens a registration modal, and one engine page anchors to a 2-field waitlist.
- CTA destination is never checkout: scroll-to-form, modal application, registration modal, self-anchor + waitlist, or next funnel step. The page's job is to start the yes, not to take payment.
- Labels restate the offer, not the click: "Join for $47.00 And Get Your Bonuses", "$497 · Start one, or scale the one you have", "CLAIM YOUR TICKET". Two-line labels put the price or condition in the sub-line.
- The label almost never varies: one "SKIP THE TRIAL. GET THE RESULT." among the longest letter's 10 otherwise identical buttons is the only deliberate deviation in the set, and it appears near the final offer.
- A full-width secondary CTA bar is used once ("ACCESS TO THE PROGRAM IS CLOSED. CLICK HERE TO JOIN THE WAITLIST.") as a persistent copy-level ask, not a sticky element.
- Availability beats gating: never hide the buy button behind watching — "people want to be able to buy apparently when they want to buy" (tested three or four times; availability won significantly). Three or more CTAs per long asset, none before the case is made, and the first ask lands early — ~9% into a two-hour VSL (18 minutes), not at the end.

### Price Presentment
- Strategy 1 — price visible early, tied to the next funnel step (legacy/program pages) — the pattern for self-serve products: "$1 for 3 Days / Try It Out For Just A Buck!", "$47.00", "$9 per month", "$37.00 for My Entrepreneur Starter Kit", "$1 for 3-Day Trial" (the app page). The price sits in an orange offer strip or H2 directly above the CTA.
- The offer strip is a reusable component: a 740×50px orange bar with 22px white text on two legacy pages, between the video and the CTA; the same strip runs full-width on the full-bleed program variant and the app page, above a 368×86px CTA.
- Price visibility is viewport-dependent: the legacy hero-card CTA is clipped 18px at 1440×900, a second legacy page shows only 6px of it, and the app page's $1 button is above the fold only at 2560×1440 — below it at 1440/768 and absent at 390, where only a social icon remains. One engine page compensates with a 134–143px bottom bar reading "Your Price Today: $37.00" at ≤768.
- Strategy 2 — price withheld until late or forever (modern/application pages) — the pattern for high-ticket offers: one modern letter states "$497 test group price" at y≈9.9k after ~10k px of letter; one engine page shows a $25,000 → $100,000 → $1,000,000 ladder at y 16k–23k after the application form; the other modern letter shows no price at all, only a $15,000 value stack; the longest letter withholds its own price and anchors against $10k–$25k consulting.
- The gate is the price: withholding pages convert through a 7- or 9-step application, not a purchase click. The application collects revenue, investable capital, phone and marketing consent; the modal is the conversion event, and the price is a page element the visitor can read but not act on directly.
- Anchoring is comparison-based, never discount-based: $47 vs "$50k–$100k" consulting, $497 vs a "$100,000 annual coaching program", $15,000 value stack itemized as $5,000 + $5,000 + $2,000 + $3,000, $10k–$25k agency alternatives. Literal "was $X, now $Y" strike-through: none of the 13.
- Value stacks are itemized line by line, not summarized: 4 line items on one modern letter, 8 bonuses on the other, 3 bonuses with a "$500 value" on the longest letter, 10 rail bullets on one engine page, 9 bonus trainings on another.
- No trial page discloses the recurring price after the trial: one legacy page sells "$1 for 3 Days" and never states the post-trial amount; the app page's $1 trial has no follow-on price on the page.
- The subscription case: a subscription app sold with the old template's mechanics — orange offer strip, countdown, repeated CTA, price in the hero — on a feature page with no application. Price visibility tracked offer type more than product type.
- A self-serve $10–$100/mo product belongs in strategy 1 — price in the hero, trial or plan link under it, no qualification gate. A high-ticket or enterprise sale belongs in strategy 2 — withhold the number behind a demo/application, but state the price band once qualified. The one element that never works is the undisclosed post-trial price, which is unworkable for a subscription.

### Urgency, Scarcity & Widgets
- Three countdowns in 13 pages: one dated to a real event ("November 14-16, 2026", static red banner that scrolls away), two evergreen (~7 minutes on one engine page, server-synced and resetting; ~35 minutes on the app page, resetting on reload). The dated countdown is the model; the two evergreen ones are manufactured urgency and must not be copied.
- The dated countdown still shows evergreen mechanics: its visible timer counts to the event while hidden copy says early-bird pricing ended October 31, 2026 — two deadlines on one page, one of them stale.
- Capacity-scarcity copy on ~6 of the 13 pages: "LIMITED TO 67 SEATS TOTAL", "4 LEFT" on the $100K tier, "Limited to 300 spots per year", "limited number of new clients... No exceptions. No waitlist.", "REGISTER NOW before space fills up", and a full-width "ACCESS TO THE PROGRAM IS CLOSED" bar. Scarcity is stated in copy, never rendered as a live counter. Use a capacity cap only if the cap is real.
- Countdown formatting is consistent: red numerals, MM:SS or DD:HH:MM:SS, repeated at the top and again near a lower CTA (25px and 16px on one engine page). The timer is a page ornament, not a sticky bar.
- Trust is carried by static proof instead: logo walls (the modern letters and the longest letter), ChatGPT-ranking screenshots (3 pages), raw message-screenshot testimonial masonry (15 images on one engine page; 12 plus 2 named on a modern letter), Wistia video testimonials (5 on one engine page), named-client photos (the longest letter). Screenshots are cheap and credible.
- Absent at desktop: sticky CTAs, exit-intent popups, chat widgets, fake purchase/notification toasts, live-viewer counters, autoplay VSLs (one dead iframe carries `autoplay=1`), on-page order bumps, guarantee badges, strikethrough anchor prices.
- The one genuine correction across viewports: sticky CTAs do exist at ≤768 on 2 of the 13 — a 69–70px bottom "CLAIM YOUR TICKET" bar after ~700–800px of scroll, and a bottom price/CTA bar from scroll 0. Both repeat the page's existing action; neither introduces a new offer. Use it as a mobile affordance, not as desktop clutter.
- Hidden or dormant widgets exist: one engine page holds 10 `.popup` divs that never fire; one legacy page holds 18 testimonial videos at 0×0; the full-bleed program variant hides its testimonial and FAQ sections; the app page hides "Meet the Founders" and a Tai quote. The visible page is the restrained one.
- Sticky navigation appears on the app page only (toolbar fixed after the hero at every size; 5 links fit to 768, hamburger at 390). One engine page adds a 53px fixed top logo bar at desktop after 18–36% scroll. No funnel page has a fixed header or a hamburger.
- No cookie walls or age gates on any page; the only floating element is the app page's 55×55 accessibility widget. Friction is removed, not added.
- The restraint lesson: conversion rests on a repeated CTA, a long argument, and static proof. The widgets that dominate generic conversion-optimization lists are absent from pages that are professionally optimized for direct response. Urgency, when present, is copy attached to a deadline — not a live feed.

### Guarantees
- Explicit guarantees appear on 4 of the 13 pages, all paid: one legacy page and three engine pages (including the longest letter) — use a guarantee where refund risk is real. Guarantee badge or boxed risk-reversal panel: none of the 13 at every viewport.
- Nine pages have none — including paid pages at an event (value stack only) and at $47, $497, $37 and $9/mo, plus the app page ($1 trial), which links only to a footer "Refund Policy". Free offers (three engine pages) carry no guarantee at all.
- Where guarantees appear, they sit inside offer or "How To Join" copy, not in a badge: a 30-day refund ("If within the first 30 days you disagree... Simply let my team know and they'll refund your money"), a "Should that happen, I'll give you a full refund", a "you get every penny back... We press one button", and a 200% guarantee ("if you do not feel you made back double what you paid, you get your money back. The one condition is that you actually use it").
- The strongest wording attaches a condition and an action ("do what we agree on the calls") — risk reversal with a usage requirement, not an unconditional promise. Keep the conditional structure; treat the 200% amount as offer-specific wording, not a rule.
- Risk reversal appears exactly where money is asked for: the four guarantees are on paid pages, and the free pages rely on disclaimers instead. Paid pages without a guarantee are common, so absence is not an error in this set — it is the norm.
- Guarantees coexist with long earnings disclaimers on the same page (two of the four guaranteeing pages): the guarantee covers the purchase, the disclaimer covers the income claim. The two are not treated as contradictory.

### Responsive & Viewport Behavior
- Across the 52 page-viewport combinations, 51 hit the requested size; one engine page at 768/390 is the exception because its overflow forces Chrome to shrink the visual viewport to 0.973–0.975 (achieved 789×1051 and 400×866).
- Mobile is a separate build, not a squeezed desktop: mobile is the majority of traffic (commonly claimed 60%, likely 80%), so the phone layout is designed separately. The measured funnel pages take the squeeze route — same content reflowed, body size held, only the headline stepping — and that route leaves 3 of the 13 with overflow at ≤768, including a hero image running 183px past the viewport. Design the 390 layout on its own terms.
- Single column is already the desktop norm: 12 of the 13 main content columns are one column; only one page keeps a persistent 875px article + 330px sidebar. The 5 two-column heroes collapse at 768 (four pages) or 390 (one), and no page uses a ≥3-column body grid. Design one column first.
- Sticky elements are nearly absent. Desktop sticky CTA: 0 of the 13. Mobile/tablet sticky CTA: 2 (one at 768/390, one at ≤768). Hamburger menus: 0 of 12 funnel pages; headers are logo-only or absent, and the footer link list is the mobile nav.

### The Feel Checklist

Score every page against these contrast rules before shipping. Left is generic modern marketing chrome; right is the direct-response signature.

- **Column:** not a 12-column dashboard grid → one centered column, 518–772px on letters (748–772 modern, 518 legacy body), up to 930px on program letters; 24–26px gutters; 800–1,350px outer container. Never a ≥3-column body; 2-column only in heroes and feature grids, collapsing at 768/390.
- **Type:** not Inter/Geist/system sans everywhere → one serif-ish heading face + one sans body: Georgia headings + Montserrat/Arial (modern), Gotham throughout (legacy), Lato 900 + Georgia (ads page), Oswald + Helvetica (squeeze page). Seven families across 13 pages, but one pairing per page, never three.
- **Scale:** not 64–96px display type → H1 33–51px, H2 20–38px, body 16–22px, line-height 1.5–1.7 (dominant 1.60–1.70); body grows with consideration (16px on $1, 22px on high-ticket), it does not shrink.
- **Spacing:** not a strict 4/8px token grid → 0px section gaps; 40–50px symmetric band padding desktop (84–100px content-to-content), 20–34px phone; media/callouts inserted with 4–28px one-sided margins; fixed px, no vh sections.
- **Accent:** not a multi-color brand palette → exactly one accent, on the CTA and the second-most-important element (red H2s on the event variant, red countdown on the webinar page, yellow hero subhead on legacy). Everything else black, white, warm gray.
- **Contrast:** CTA text contrast ranges 2.3:1–7.0:1 and 10 of the 13 miss 4.5:1. Build at the 7:1 end (#25652F on white), not the 2.3:1 end.
- **Surfaces:** not elevated cards with 16–24px radii → flat tinted panels (#fffafa, #f6f6f6, #f7f7f1) or 1–2px bordered panels; 0–3px borders; mostly 0–8px radii; 25–50px pills only on engine pages (copywriting page, webinar page); the 15px legacy hero card is the one large radius. Backgrounds separate sections; borders rarely do.
- **No effects:** no gradients anywhere; shadows on only 4 pages (a CTA, a form card, FAQ cards); no glow, glassmorphism, or layered shadows. A floating form card may have a soft shadow; panels do not.
- **No iconography:** icon bullets on 2 pages, feature icons on 2, directional arrows on 1; star ratings and payment/SSL badges on none. Lists are disc, decimal or unmarked; the scanning aid is a bold "Lead-in:" on 6 of the 13 pages, not a checkmark.
- **CTA grammar:** one label per page, repeated 2–10 times, never varied ("CLAIM YOUR TICKET", "GRAB YOUR FREE SPOT"); 44–86px tall; uppercase and tracked on engine pages (20px/900, 1.2–2px), 18px/700 on modern green, two-line on legacy orange; one action per page; destination is form, modal, waitlist or next step — never checkout.
- **Alignment:** centered headings and heroes, left body copy; never center 300-word paragraphs. Headings are section labels — 17–19 H2s on a long letter, no H3 hierarchy needed.
- **Bands:** white ↔ warm-gray rgb(247,247,241) on modern letters; black shell + white boxes on legacy; 1px rules and no bands on the ads page; touching full-bleed bands on engine pages. Alternate by background, never by card shadow.
- **Text dominance:** media is proof or product, not decoration — 116–254 words per asset on letters, 2.6–30% media share on most pages, one image every 300 words or 1,500–2,500px. No image-led hero on the letter templates; the video is proof inside a text argument.
- **Media behavior:** 16:9 VSL 694–772px wide, poster-first, paused, never autoplay on a live page; composed speaker/title-card posters, never a mid-speech frame; testimonial screenshots kept at native aspect ratios, uncropped.
- **Copy rhythm:** 1–3-sentence paragraphs averaging 21–34 words; H1 6–37 words; subhead = mechanism + effort removal; 5–10-item lists absorbing deliverables; one promise, one offer.
- **Repetition:** CTA every 1,000–2,500px of scroll; proof near the top; value stack before price; risk reversal inside offer copy; FAQ before the final CTA; legal footer last. Urgency only as copy attached to a real deadline.

**Anti-patterns — reject on sight:**
- Elevated shadow cards, 16–24px radii, floating panels.
- Gradient heroes, glassmorphism, dark-mode neon, glowing buttons.
- An icon on every bullet, feature-icon rows, star ratings, trust badges, payment logos.
- Multi-color palettes, two competing CTA colors, a secondary filled CTA.
- 64–96px display H1s, centered body text, 12-column marketing grids.
- Full-bleed image heroes with overlay copy on the letter templates; autoplay VSLs.
- Generic modern chrome: six-link nav bars, mega-menus, chat bubbles, cookie walls, live counters, toasts, exit popups.
- Sticky desktop CTA bars; icon-heavy "features" grids as the main argument; big rounded pill buttons on long-form letters.

If a block would look at home in a generic modern template, it is wrong for this system. If it would look at home in a 2010 long-form sales letter — flat, quiet, text-led, one green/orange/blue button — it is probably right.

### Choosing the Page Shape
- **Keep for any product page:** single centered column 518–772px, 16–22px body, 1.5–1.7 line-height, 1–3-sentence paragraphs, one CTA label repeated every 1,000–2,500px, first-viewport conversion action, one-question-per-screen forms, scarcity only when real, countdowns only against genuine deadlines, application/waitlist for high-ticket, guarantees where refund risk is real, raw screenshot proof, numbered value stacks, objection-answering FAQ, mobile sticky bar repeating the existing action.
- **Drop for low-consideration or self-serve products:** long hero videos (short demos are the ceiling), offer-strip price theater, undisclosed post-trial pricing, hidden pricing under ~$100/mo, scarcity theater, celebrity proof, salutation-letter voice, fixed-px heroes, overflow, blocked zoom.
- **Offer shape decides the page:** the short, image-led, price-visible page fits understood, low-consideration products; the long letter fits abstract, high-consideration ones; modern pages converge toward the short pattern while the letter survives for high-ticket sales.
- **App-page pattern:** the same visual grammar — offer strip, countdown, four repeated CTAs — with the letter replaced by four feature cards, three "why" columns, one testimonial poster, a sticky nav, and product screenshots; 4,076–6,581px and 409 words, shorter and lighter than a long-form letter (8,872–25,205px, 794–4,325 words).
- **Adapt the content form:** keep the page geometry and the CTA cadence; replace a long VSL with a short demo, a value stack with a feature/outcome list, a testimonial wall with named customer quotes or review screenshots, and an application gate with a demo form only when the deal size justifies it.
- **Hold the visual constants:** one accent colour, one CTA label, the first-viewport ask, the 518–772px measure, and flat panels.
- The *content* differs by offer; the *geometry* — measure, spacing, type, chunking, CTA cadence — holds for every page.
